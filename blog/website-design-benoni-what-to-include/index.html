Website Design in Benoni: What a Good Business Website Should Include

A plain-English guide to planning a business website in Benoni, from the pages you need to the questions worth asking before you accept a quote.

7 min read · Updated August 2026

A good business website does not need to be complicated. It needs to make it easy for the right person to understand what you do, trust you, and get in touch.

For many businesses in Benoni and the East Rand, the first useful question is not “Which website package should I buy?” It is “What does my customer need to see before they call, visit, or ask for a quote?”

Start with the job your website needs to do

A plumber, an engineering company, a school, and an online shop do not need the same website. A service business may need clear service pages, an area list, real photos, reviews, and a fast quote form. A shop needs product information, payment choices, delivery details, and help after a sale.

Simple rule: if a visitor cannot tell what you offer, who it is for, and what to do next within a few seconds, the page needs work.

The pages most small businesses need

  • Home page: a clear introduction and one main next step.
  • Service pages: one page for each important service, written in the customer’s language.
  • About page: who you are, where you work, and why customers can trust you.
  • Proof: real project photos, approved reviews, or short case studies.
  • Contact page: phone, WhatsApp, email, location, opening hours, and a working form.
  • Privacy and terms pages: especially important when you collect enquiries online.

What should be included in a website quote?

Ask for a written list of what is included. A useful quote normally covers page count, design, mobile layout, content support, contact forms, basic search setup, hosting, domain responsibility, training, and support after launch.

It is also worth asking what is not included. Examples include professional photography, copywriting, paid advertising, product uploads, advanced booking systems, payment gateway fees, and ongoing maintenance.

When do you need an online shop or a web app?

An online shop is for selling products or taking orders online. A web app is different. It may include a client portal, booking workflow, staff dashboard, calculator, or a process that your business currently manages with spreadsheets and messages.

If you are not sure which you need, start with the customer journey. Write down what happens from the first enquiry to the final payment or handover. That usually makes the right next step clearer.

Questions to ask before you choose a web designer

  1. Who will own the domain, website files, and content after launch?
  2. How will the site work on a phone?
  3. What do you need from me before the project can start?
  4. How will enquiries reach me, and who checks that the form works?
  5. What support is available after launch?
  6. Can I see work that is similar to my type of business?

Keep the words natural

You do not need to repeat “website design Benoni” in every sentence. Use the place name where it helps a reader understand the area you serve. Then explain the service clearly. Search engines and people both respond better to useful pages than awkward repetition.
