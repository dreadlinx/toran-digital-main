Full Vehicle Wrap vs Partial Branding: Which Is Right for Your Business?

A simple guide to choosing between full wraps, partial wraps, decals, and fleet branding for business vehicles in Gauteng.

7 min read · Updated August 2026

Your vehicles can do more than get people and equipment from one place to another. They can help customers recognise your business, find your contact details, and feel more confident about calling you.

The right option is not always a full wrap. It depends on the vehicle, your brand, how long you will keep the vehicle, your budget, and how much coverage you need.

What is the difference?

OptionWhat it usually meansOften suits
Decals and letteringEssential details such as logo, phone number, website, and a simple message.Smaller budgets or vehicles that need a clean, practical look.
Partial wrapVinyl graphics cover selected panels and work with the vehicle’s original colour.Businesses that want stronger impact without covering the whole vehicle.
Full wrapMost visible areas of the vehicle are covered with a complete design.Campaign vehicles, strong brand launches, or high-visibility work.
Fleet brandingA repeatable design system for several vehicles.Teams that need consistent branding across vans, bakkies, trucks, or cars.

Choose based on the job the vehicle needs to do

If your customers need to recognise you while you are parked at their home or business, clear contact details and a simple service message may matter more than a busy design. If the vehicle is a major part of your marketing, a full or partial wrap can create a stronger visual impression.

For a fleet, consistency matters. Every vehicle should use the same logo rules, colour, contact details, and placement so that people recognise the business quickly.

What affects a vehicle branding quote?

  • Vehicle make, model, and condition.
  • Whether you need decals, a partial wrap, a full wrap, or a fleet rollout.
  • How much artwork already exists and whether design work is needed.
  • Number of vehicles and whether they need the same layout.
  • Materials, finishes, reflective elements, and installation requirements.
  • Your preferred installation date and how long the vehicle can be off the road.

Keep the design easy to read

People often see a branded vehicle for only a few seconds. Your logo, service, phone number, and website should be easy to read from a sensible distance. Too much copy can make the important details disappear.

Before you ask for a quote: send a photo of the vehicle, its make and model, the number of vehicles, your logo files, the services you want to promote, and any deadline. This helps create a more useful starting discussion.

Look after the finished work

Ask the installer for care guidance based on the material and finish used. Good care, sensible cleaning, and quick attention to damage help keep the branding looking professional for longer.
