Is Vehicle Branding Worth It? The ROI for Johannesburg Businesses

How to actually think about the return on a vehicle wrap, instead of guessing.

6 min read · Updated August 2026

A vehicle wrap is a once-off cost that keeps advertising for as long as the vehicle is on the road - which makes it one of the few marketing spends that doesn't need a recurring budget to keep working. Whether it's "worth it" for your business comes down to a few concrete factors, not a single number that applies to everyone.

Why the cost-per-impression math favours wraps

Outdoor advertising industry research (from bodies like the Out of Home Advertising Association) has consistently found that vehicle advertising delivers one of the lowest costs per thousand impressions of any advertising format, largely because the vehicle keeps generating impressions on every trip, at no extra cost, for years after the wrap is installed. Compare that to a billboard or a print ad, where you pay again every month for the same exposure.

The real driver of ROI isn't the wrap itself - it's how much the vehicle actually drives. A delivery bakkie doing daily rounds across Johannesburg earns its wrap back far faster than a vehicle that mostly sits in a yard.

What changes the return

  • Daily mileage and routes: more time on well-travelled roads means more impressions.
  • Fleet size: wrapping multiple vehicles compounds visibility across more of the city at once.
  • Design clarity: a wrap that's readable at a glance (big, simple, high-contrast) converts attention into recall far better than a cluttered one.
  • Full wrap vs. partial: full wraps maximise coverage and impact; partial wraps and decals cost less and still work well for vehicles with tighter budgets.
  • How long you keep the vehicle: the payback period shortens the longer the vehicle stays in service.

Beyond direct leads: the trust effect

A professionally wrapped fleet also does something harder to measure but genuinely valuable: it signals that a business is established and takes itself seriously. For trades and service businesses especially, a branded vehicle parked outside a job site works as a passive reference for neighbours and passers-by, in a way an unbranded vehicle simply can't.

A simple way to think about payback

Instead of asking "will this pay for itself," ask "how many new customers would this need to bring in before it's paid for itself, given what one customer is typically worth to my business?" For most service businesses in Gauteng, that number turns out to be small relative to how many people actually see a well-driven, well-wrapped vehicle over a year.
