How Much Does It Cost to Build a Mobile App in South Africa?

A simple guide to the main things that affect mobile app cost in South Africa, including scope, platforms, features, testing, launch, and support.

8 min read · Updated August 2026

There is no honest one-size-fits-all price for a mobile app. A simple app with a clear job can be very different from an app that needs accounts, payments, live location, a staff dashboard, and links to other systems.

The most useful way to plan an app is to begin with the problem. What should a customer, driver, staff member, or manager be able to do more easily when the app is live?

What changes the cost of an app?

DecisionWhy it matters
Number of featuresEvery feature needs planning, design, build work, testing, and support.
iPhone, Android, or bothBuilding for one platform is different from supporting both platforms.
Accounts and permissionsCustomer, staff, and admin access need different rules and testing.
Payments and outside systemsPayment, booking, stock, maps, or accounting links add work and responsibility.
Back-office toolsMany apps also need a dashboard for managing orders, people, or content.

Start with an MVP, not every idea at once

An MVP is the smallest useful version of a product. It is not a rushed version. It is a focused version that helps you test the core job before you pay for every extra feature.

For example, a booking app may begin with availability, booking requests, reminders, and an admin view. It might add loyalty points, chat, advanced reports, and extra payment options later when there is a clear reason to do so.

Native or cross-platform: what is the difference?

Native apps are built specifically for iPhone or Android. Cross-platform apps share more of the same code across both. Neither choice is automatically better. The right choice depends on the kind of app, performance needs, device features, budget, and future plans.

Ask for a recommendation in plain English: explain what the app must do, who will use it, what systems it connects to, and what must work on day one. A good project brief is more useful than asking for a price before the scope is clear.

Do not forget the work around the app

An app project may include discovery, screen design, content, technical build, testing, data protection, store accounts, launch material, training, and support. These are not optional extras. They are part of making an app usable in the real world.

What to bring to an app discussion

  • The problem you want to solve and who has it.
  • A simple list of the first three things users must be able to do.
  • Any existing website, spreadsheet, form, or system the app must work with.
  • Your preferred launch date and realistic budget range.
  • Examples of apps you like and what you like about them.

After launch

Apps need care after launch. Operating systems change, customers find edge cases, and businesses change their processes. Discuss support, fixes, changes, app-store access, and who owns the accounts before the work begins.
